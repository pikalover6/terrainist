## Iteration 3 — the orphans get diagnosed, and the hypothesis is refuted

**The headline is a negative result, and it is worth more than the fix would
have been.** Iteration 3's target was the orphan stair treads (271 on the
hillside fixture, 3,421 on the steep one), on the standing theory that the seam
builders publish landings for flights that do not exist. **They do not.** The
orphan columns were measured at the column: 97% of them stand at *exactly* the
level their own emitter declared, and every one is attributed to a
`street:segment:*` — whole streets and whole flights, not landings.

**What actually severs them is the ground contract's own rank order.** A flight
declares its tread band as `street.network`, rank 80, tier C. A quarter's
platform plane is `quarter.plane`, **rank 15, tier A**. Where they overlap the
flight loses, always — so the level it publishes on that column is a level it
never gets, and what ships is a flight with a notch cut through it. Proved on
`site-plan-hillside`: the carriage spine `sp0` declares y = 111 across
z = 56…60; the resolver's winner at (94…100, 58) is `world.hill_town#pad@26`,
`quarter.plane`, at **109**. Seven columns, two blocks down, orphaning **223
columns of spine**. The same shape explains `dn1_96`, `dn1_32`, and the 4-block
break in `dn2_32` that separates two 600-column terraces on the steep fixture.

**Two fixes were built and measured, and both were rejected on their own
numbers** (so the next attempt does not pay for them again):

1. *Pin the tread law to the decided columns; refuse the run that cannot meet
   them.* It works — hillside orphans **271 → 9**, `unservedFaces` → 0 on both
   fixtures, troy's cliff census 6,966 → 6,942 and every `streetFlank` row down
   with `buildingSeats` untouched. It is also not shippable: a one-column notch
   two blocks deep is infeasible by arithmetic, so nearly every contested flight
   refuses whole and the steep fixture loses **a third of its paving**
   (4,369 → 2,927 columns; the tread mix 323 → 35 half-treads). Deleting the
   fabric is not a fix.
2. *Bend where you can, keep today's answer where you cannot.* Monotone, safe,
   and worth nothing: the hillside does not move one column and the steep
   fixture moves three. **There is no bend** — 111, 109, 111 is a two-block
   riser whichever way a 1-Lipschitz law is bent.

So the lever is upstream of both the stair and the tread law, and it is a
design decision rather than a patch: **either a quarter's plane yields where a
street crosses it, or the street router does not route across a plane it cannot
