## §B The causes

1. **F27 — the icon is authored at house scale.** The bell pavilion is
   1.27× the houses' height; the pit heads are `building.grammar` huts; the
   ferry rig is a low frame; the monastery's assembly hall is 1.7× tall on
   a mountain that is 200× taller. The model writes the thing the prompt
   screams at the scale of a building, and nothing in the kit's program or
   landmark sections says *an icon is built to dominate* (T1: scale,
   height, prominence over the ordinary buildings). The whale — written
   huge — is the proof it can. Kit-teaching; the metric agrees with the eye
   in all four cases, so F15's threshold question is not what failed here.
2. **F26 — the fjord stayed dry and nothing said so.** The two-villages
   document carved `fjord_channel` (`valley`, width 38, depth 22, course
   down the middle, `flooded: "auto"`) between its two districts, but the
   heightfield stands at 95–117 there and a 22-deep valley never reaches
   sea level 63: the floor measured 95 at z −20, 80 at z 50. `auto` means
   "water if below the sea", so the compiler carved a dry valley and issued
   no diagnostic — the compile-feedback rounds had nothing to react to, and
   the prompt's *joined only by a rope ferry* became a ferry beside a
   mountain. `LOAM-T113 CARVE_DRY` stayed silent because one wet column
   was enough for it. Fixed in this unit: `LOAM-I502 CARVE_MOSTLY_DRY` —
   `reportDryCarves` now walks the carve's own floor samples and names a
   `flooded: "auto"` carve below half wet. On this document: *"48 of 346
   points along its floor reach the sea (14 %), and along the rest the
   floor stands up to 31 blocks above sea level."*
3. **Terrain-carried icons are unmeasurable** (the caldera, the open cut):
   the metric compares buildings; a lake or a cut has none. Logged with F24.
4. `W517 INTENT_FORM_PACK_ERA` (wilds_camps in the Bronze Age) — the T9
   guard firing as designed; the model's pack choice, not a new class.
