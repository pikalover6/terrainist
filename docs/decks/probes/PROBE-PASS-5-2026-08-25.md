## §B The frozen fjord that cannot freeze

The prompt asked for a trading town on a frozen fjord, longships hauled onto
the ice. The compiler has snow (`snowLine`, per column, slope-gated) and no
ice: a water column's surface is water in every climate, so "frozen" is not
a thing a document can ask for. The model wrote the fjord as water, the
town's footprints fell below sea level (`E406`, none of 97 could stand), the
solver placed it at the least-violating spot — the summit — and the
longships sought a waterline (`T228`) that was never ice.

- **F32 — no frozen water.** A new cause (a climate feature the grammar
  lacks, like F30's seat over water): in a freezing climate the water
  surface should be ice, and a footprint on ice is a legal seat for a prop
  or a light structure. The fix is a compiler unit — `ICE_ON_FROZEN_WATER`,
  the top water block written as ice where the snow line is at or below the
  water (or `climate.snow: "always"`), a note counting the columns — staged
  under law 5 (no anchor or fixture has a freezing climate; the viking
  document is the attribution), then six more probes for G4.

## §C The turtle, and the seat the grammar lacks

`turtle_beached_shelf`, `turtle_shell_ridge_north/south`: the shell is
terrain — raises — with the houses seated on it; `turtle_head` a program;
`flipper_rigs` and `drying_scaffolds` programs whose site rule took no site

to seat a building on anything but ground, it made the thing ground. Same
cause, a second surface — a program's surface rather than water — and P8
widens to "a seat that is not ground" (piles to a bed; a footprint on a
program's top). Not a new class by the pre-registered rule.
