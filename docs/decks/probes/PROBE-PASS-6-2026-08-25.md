## §B What failed, and whose it is

Four of the six failures or halves have one cause: **a structure seats only
on flat ground.** The shanties could not stand on the ice (F32 gave them a
frozen lake and F30 denies them a seat on it), the pylon line could not
stand on the slope, the erratics and the iceberg were refused on ground
"that falls away like a cliff", the steamer could not moor on water. F30
was written for water (the flood delta) and widened for a program's top
(the turtle); it widens again for slopes and ice. **P8 — a seat that is not
flat ground** — now carries four probe failures across three passes and is
the first thing the Run recommends after itself (§D).

## §C The glacier: the land half of the ice gap

F32 taught the compiler that water can be ice. A glacier is ice as *land* —
a tongue filling a valley, a snout, moraines — and no terrain verb makes
one; the model reached for programs on a mountainside and the seat rule
refused them. The same gap as F32, its land half: **P11 — ice landforms**
(a `glacier` verb: a raise of packed ice down a valley with a snout and a
meltwater lake; moraines as ridges), kit-taught after the verb. Not a new
cause by the pre-registered rule.

## §D G4

No new failure class: every FAIL's cause is F30/P8 or F32's family (P11),
both open as written proposals, and the pre-registered rule counts those
as known. **G4 is met** by its letter. Its substance is this: the probes
stopped finding compiler bugs two passes ago and now find the grammar's
edges — a seat on water, ice, a slope, a program; an ice landform — which
are features, and P8 is the one worth building first.
