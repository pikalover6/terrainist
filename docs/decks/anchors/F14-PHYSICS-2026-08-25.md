physics findings on shipped worlds; one physics unit, probe first" (D26).

world **readback** validator) over the thirteen anchors and fixtures

## §B The lichen: a legal floor, an instrument that could not see it

Every lichen finding is `glow_lichen[down=true]` on top of a full cube —
`decorate.ts` lays floor lichen on grass with its single face `down`
(bbox of the 12,444: y 66…91, 5,664 of them at y 91, the pirate cove's
ground). In the game, `MultifaceBlock.canAttachTo` accepts any sturdy
face, floors included. The lint's rule (written from `VineBlock`, which
has no `down`) skipped `up` and `down` when looking for an anchor and
judged `up` alone against the ceiling — so a floor lichen could never be
anchored and was always "a flat plate in mid-air". Fixed: `down` anchors
on a sturdy block below like the four sides; the vine-only "carried by the
strand above" clause stays for the sides only.

## §C The sea lantern: a full cube named like a lamp

`sea_lantern` ends in "lantern". `stdlib` `support.ts` classed it as a
lamp (`NEEDS_GROUND`, `supportDirection` → "below" or "above"), and the
lint's own lantern rule tested `name.endsWith("lantern")` too — so every
sea lantern set into a wall or a mast on the alien farm, the Hellenist
worlds and the unicorn citadel was "stands on air" and "its support chain
does not reach a solid block". Fixed in both places; the hanging-chain
rule (`hanging === "true"`) never matched it, and the walking agent's own
skip list (`physics.ts` ≈ 1318) is left as it is (D63).
`stdlib/test/support-sea-lantern.test.ts` pins the classification.
