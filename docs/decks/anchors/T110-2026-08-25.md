# F1 — `LOAM-T110 UNSTABLE_FLUID` (unit 14, 2026-08-25)

The emit refusal the Run's gates kept exposing: "N fluid blocks would flow on
the first tick" — `checkFluidStability` (`terrain/validate.ts`) finds a fluid
column whose four-neighbour's surface (ground, or its own fluid) sits below
the fluid's top: a dry hole beside water. The CLI calls it a compiler bug,
not a document problem, and it refused five authored documents in the Run:
the before-sample's railway town (71 blocks), the E1 arms' metropolis three
times (2, 7, 396) and redwood camp once (8). Compiled with `--allow-unstable`
and traced pass by pass (verbatim in §C), the five are **three mechanisms**:

| document | blocks | the water | what sat below it | mechanism |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| railway_town (before-sample) | 71 | a `basin water:true` pool at y 54 | the same columns rewritten to y 51 by a sealess `river`'s demoted pond, settled second | **F-B** `settleFluidPool` writes unconditionally — the second pool lowers the first (`terrain/columns.ts`) |
| metropolis (e1-modules-1) | 2 | the sea at 63 | a dry below-sea column at ground 62 beside it | **F-A** `computeOceanMask`: the fill refuses a `flooded: "never"` column; the repair floods it but never continues the fill past it (`stdlib/classify/index.ts`) |
| redwood_camp (e1-modules-1) | 8 | the sea | same | F-A |
| metropolis (e1-core-3) | 7 | the sea | same | F-A |
| metropolis (e1-modules-2) | 396 | a `basin water:true` lake at 72 | a partly-submerged terrace pad graded to 71 whose ground claim drains the lake's columns — `mostlyWater` is a majority vote (`layout/platforms.ts`), the ground contract treats an omitted fluid as dry (`ground-contract.ts`), the driver writes it (`ground-driver.ts`) | **#5 — a subsystem**: nothing in the ground contract asks about the water standing next door |

Shared: a water surface written without consulting the water already
standing beside it — twice in the terrain's own inputs (F-A, F-B), once in
a consumer (#5). Not the check, not the documents.

## A. Two switches, landed off

- **`OCEAN_FILL_CONTINUES`** (`packages/stdlib/src/classify/index.ts`): a
  column the repair had to flood joins the fill's own queue and the fill
  drains from it under its own rules. Pinned on a synthetic 7 × 7 field by
  `packages/compiler/test/fluid-stability-fixes.test.ts`: today the column
  behind the flooded gap stays dry; on, the sea reaches it; with no `never`
  mask the two are identical.
- **`POOL_NEVER_LOWERS`** (`packages/compiler/src/terrain/columns.ts`): a
  column already holding the same fluid above a pool's level keeps its
  surface; a second pool may only raise it.

**Off-state:** fourteen of fourteen law-5 worlds payload-identical.

**Trial at both on** (local): railway_town **clean** (71 → 0), metropolis
e1-modules-1 **clean**, redwood_camp **clean**, metropolis e1-core-3
**clean**, metropolis e1-modules-2 still **396** (#5, the subsystem). And
**all fourteen law-5 worlds payload-identical with the switches on** — the
fixes touch only a document with a stranded `never` column or overlapping
pools; none of the walked worlds has either.

## B. Disposition

- Flip both in the next unit (nothing moves; the four documents compile).
- **#5 → proposal P4:** a wet-neighbour constraint in the ground contract —
  a pad or platform whose claim would lower a column beside standing water
  below that water's surface is refused or raised to it, the same rule the
  election's `waterFloor` already states for platforms, applied at the
  ground claim. Until then a `basin water:true` beside a stepped quarter can
  drain into a pad.
- F1 stays open on #5 only.

## C. The probe, verbatim

# T110 probe — five refusals, attributed

Method: `--allow-unstable` compile of each doc with an env-guarded dump in
`packages/compiler/dist/terrain/validate.js` (ALL unstable columns + their four
neighbours' `ground`/`fluidTop`/`fluidKind`), plus env-guarded `T110TRACE`
probes in `dist/terrain/compile.js` at eight pipeline points (after
`buildColumnPlan`, caves, structures, programs, clearing, vegetation, urban
floor, biomes) and a `T110BASIN` probe in `dist/terrain/columns.js`. All three
dist files restored byte-for-byte (shas at the bottom).

The check itself is sound. `checkFluidStability`
(`packages/compiler/src/terrain/validate.ts:56-95`) is exact: a fluid column is
unstable iff some 4-neighbour's `max(ground, fluidTop)` is below its own
`fluidTop`. Every witness below is a real drainable block. No false positives.

## 1. `before-sample/railway_town.doc.json` — 71 blocks, 27 columns

- Witnesses: x∈[-18,-5], z∈[-11,-4], every one `top=54`, `floor=51..52`.
  Unstable already **at `after-buildColumnPlan`** (71) — no later pass involved.
- Water source: two overlapping basin pools. `wharf_basin`
  (`verb: "basin", water: true, radius 42, depth 6`) settles at `waterY=54`
  over 5110 columns. `main_canal_channel` (`verb: "river"`, `"coast"` anchor)
  reaches no sea, so `resolvePondChains` demotes it (LOAM-T112) to one pond,
  `main_canal_channel#pond1`, `waterY=51`, 18 columns.
- Probe: `T110BASIN` shows column (-13,-8) is in **both** pools. The world
  confirms it — gravel to y=50, water at 51, air at 52-54, one block from a
  column that is water to 54.
- Mechanism: `buildColumnPlan` walks `input.basins` in order
  (`packages/compiler/src/terrain/columns.ts:284-287`) and `settleFluidPool`
  writes `fluidTop[idx] = level` **unconditionally**
  (`packages/compiler/src/terrain/columns.ts:757`). The pond runs second and
  *lowers* 18 columns the basin had already filled to 54 down to 51, punching
  a pit inside a finished lake. `settleFluidPool`'s own settle passes, because
  its `topAt` sees the neighbours at 54 (≥ 51) and calls them walls — the settle
  proves the *pond* stable and never asks whether it broke the pool it landed in.
- Contributing: `gatherPond` probes with `topAt: floorAt`
  (`packages/stdlib/src/edits/index.ts:1498`) — bare terrain height, blind to
  any basin `waterY` already resolved — and its bed columns are not excluded
  from a basin footprint.

## 4. `e1-core-3/metropolis_hideout.doc.json` — 7 blocks

One mechanism, identical in all three. Witnesses (d2 (164,63,76), (165,63,77);
d3 eight columns near (130,63,-145); d4 seven columns along z=-1, x∈[-180,-174])
all read `top=63` (= seaLevel), `floor=62`, and the destabilizing neighbour is a
**dry** column with `ground=62`, `fluidKind=NONE`. Unstable already at
`after-buildColumnPlan` in every case — no structure pass involved.

- Probe at d4 (-177,-1) vs (-177,0):
  `-177,-1 v=62.4896 ocean=1 noFlood=1` / `-177,0 v=62.9523 ocean=0 noFlood=0`.
  Same shape at d2 (164,76 ocean=1 noFlood=1 / 165,76 ocean=0 noFlood=0) and d3.
- Every one of these three documents carries a `flooded: "never"` carve
  (`collapse_fissure` + `overgrown_canal`; `forest_creek`;
  `sunken_transit_trench`), which is what populates `edits.noFlood`.
- Mechanism: `computeOceanMask`
  (`packages/stdlib/src/classify/index.ts:106-165`). The main edge flood fill
  refuses to enter a `noFlood` column (`:121`), so a below-sea pocket reachable
  only *through* the carve is never reached. The fixed-point repair then floods
  blocked columns that border water (`:144-163`) — but its guard is
  `!blocked(idx) → continue` (`:151`), so it only ever flips **blocked**
  columns and **never re-runs the ordinary fill outward from them**. The
  newly-wet `noFlood` column at v=62.49 therefore sits next to an unblocked
  below-sea column at v=62.95 that stays dry: `ground = floor(62.95) = 62`
  (`packages/compiler/src/terrain/columns.ts:197`), sea water at 63 beside it.
- The repair's own docstring states the invariant it fails to keep: "a dry
  below-sea column touching ocean water would leave that water with an exposed
  air face."

## 5. `e1-modules-2/metropolis_hideout.doc.json` — 396 blocks, 162 columns

The only one whose instability is made **after** the terrain.

- Witnesses: a long run along z=51, x∈[-78,…], `top=72`, `floor=70`,
  destabilizer is `+z` at `ground=71`, `fluidKind=NONE`.
- Trace: `after-buildColumnPlan` unstable **0**; `after-structures` unstable
  **396**. At (-78,52): `g=71 f=72 k=1` → `g=71 f=71 k=0`. The structures pass
  *drained* a lake column without raising it.
- Water source: `flooded_subway_sinkhole` (`verb: "basin", water: true,
  radius 44, depth 10`), pool `waterY=72`, `lakeMask=1` on all three probes.
- What cut it: the layout pads moved these columns (`pristine=70.39 →
  padded=71`) and the report puts a placement `terrace_-78_52`
  (`x0:-78,z0:52,x1:-53,z1:60`) with its corner exactly on the witness.
- Mechanism: a platform grades unless it is **`mostlyWater`** — a majority vote
  over its bench columns (`packages/compiler/src/layout/platforms.ts:400`,
  applied at `:482` and again at `:1014-1016`, and reported as LOAM-I526
  PLATFORM_SUBMERGED for the platforms that *do* trip it). A terrace that is
  only *partly* over the lake grades anyway, and its minority water columns are
  carried into a `precinct`/`quarter.plane` ground claim at y=71. A
  `GroundClaim` with no `fluid` field means "dry, and `fluidTop` follows `y`"
  (`packages/compiler/src/layout/ground-contract.ts:132-145`), and
  `GroundDriver.commit` writes exactly that —
  `packages/compiler/src/layout/ground-driver.ts:388-390`
  (`fluidTop[k] = resolved.fluidTop[k]; fluidKind[k] = resolved.fluidKind[k]`).
  Nothing in the claim, the resolver or the commit asks whether a 4-neighbour
  still holds water above `y`.

## The shared mechanism (≤10 lines)

Two mechanisms, one shape. Every refusal is a **water surface written without
consulting the water already standing beside it**:

1. **The verb side (d1) and the hydrology side (d2–d4)** — both inside
   `buildColumnPlan`'s inputs. d1: two pools overlap and the second overwrites
   the first *downward*. d2–d4: the ocean fill's `noFlood` repair floods the
   blocked column but does not continue the fill past it, stranding a dry
   below-sea column against the sea.
2. **The consumer side (d5)** — a ground claim drains a lake column to the
   platform level because "omitted fluid means dry".

In none of the five is the check at fault, and in none of the five is the
document at fault: `flooded: "never"` beside a coast, and a `basin water: true`
overlapping a `river` or a terrace, are all legal authoring. The CLI's "this is
a compiler bug, not a document problem" is correct.

## Fix candidates

**F-A (d2, d3, d4 — 3 of 5, the cheapest and the most contained).**
`packages/stdlib/src/classify/index.ts:144-163`: make the repair a continuation
of the flood fill rather than a separate blocked-only sweep — when a blocked
column is flooded, push it onto the same queue and let the ordinary fill run
from it (which will then flood unblocked below-sea neighbours too). ~6 lines.
Reach: every world with a `flooded: "never"` carve whose pocket touches the sea
— byte-moving for those, byte-identical for every world with no `noFlood` mask
(the repair loop is skipped entirely when `noFlood === undefined`) and for every
world whose `noFlood` columns are landlocked. Needs the §3 law 5 switch.

**F-B (d1).** `packages/compiler/src/terrain/columns.ts:757`: never lower an
existing surface — `if (fluidKind[idx] !== NONE && fluidTop[idx] > level)
continue;` inside `settleFluidPool`'s write loop. Two lines, and it makes the
pool loop order-independent. A better but larger version resolves overlapping
`out.basins` in `stdlib` before materialization (drop or raise a pond whose bed
is inside a settled basin, `packages/stdlib/src/edits/index.ts:1466-1502`).
Reach: any world with two water bodies whose footprints overlap — rare, so a
narrow baseline move.

**F-C (d5) — this one is a subsystem, not a patch.** The ground contract has no
"a neighbour holds water above me" constraint anywhere: not in `GroundClaim`,
not in the resolver, not in `commit`. The honest minimal version is a guard at
`packages/compiler/src/layout/ground-driver.ts:388-390` refusing to drop a
column's `fluidTop` below a wet 4-neighbour's — but that leaves a one-column
water wall against a graded terrace, which is a taste problem, not a physics
one. The real fix is `mostlyWater` (`platforms.ts:400`) becoming *any*-water for
the grading decision, or a revetment claim on the lake edge. Largest reach of
the three (every settlement whose platforms touch a lake or a coast) and the
one that should not ship before an instrument verdict.

Recommended order: F-A first (3 of 5 documents, smallest diff, cleanest
byte-identity argument), then F-B, then F-C as a designed unit.
